# Canonical discographies for seven Argentine tango orchestras

Every reissue-tagged date in the existing database is confirmed erroneous. Research across el-recodo.com, tango.info, TodoTango.com, TangoSparks, and Héctor Palazzo's discographic blog establishes authoritative recording counts, date ranges, and singer rosters for all seven directors. Several of the user's initial estimates (notably Malerba's count and label) required significant correction. Below is a director-by-director canonical reference.

---

## 1. Ricardo Malerba recorded only 43 titles, not 150

**Critical corrections to initial assumptions.** The estimated ~150 recordings on RCA Victor is wrong on both counts. All authoritative sources — el-recodo.com, TodoTango (Néstor Pinsón biography), and Palazzo's blog — agree on **43 commercial recordings: 38 on Odeón (1941–1957) plus 5 on Music Hall (1957)**. The "RCA Victor" attribution likely stems from BMG/RCA "Reliquias" reissue CDs. His death year of **1974** (June 29) is confirmed across all sources; 1983 is erroneous.

**Recording span:** September 25, 1941 – 1957

**Complete singer roster:**
- **Orlando Medina** — 22 recordings (1941–1944), primary vocalist
- **Antonio Maida** — 5 recordings (1944–1945)
- **Alberto Sánchez** — 7 recordings (1956–1957)
- **Roberto Soler/Solar** — 3 recordings (1957, Music Hall)
- **Instrumental** — ~5 recordings

**Reissue verification:** The two songs tagged "year=2017" are digital reissues. Palazzo's blog post was published June 5, 2017, which may be the metadata contamination source. No recordings exist after 1957.

## 2. Domingo Federico's RCA Victor masters were largely destroyed

**~185 unique recordings** exist across all sources, far fewer than the 429 entries on tango.info (which includes reissue duplicates). Federico recorded for **RCA Victor (1944–c.1957), Odeón (1963), Embassy, and Rosafón**. A Colombian executive appointed to head RCA Victor Argentina destroyed the majority of Federico's masters — of 100+ recorded compositions, only **~18–20 RCA masters survived** in playable condition.

**Recording span:** April 10, 1944 – c.1975 (two late vals recordings)

**Complete singer roster with track counts:**
- **Carlos Vidal** — 104 appearances (1944–1969), primary vocalist across entire career
- **Oscar Larroca** — 46 appearances (1945–1948), often in duo with Vidal (29 duets)
- **Armando Moreno** — 29 appearances (through 1956)
- **Rubén Sánchez** — 28 appearances (1960–1966, Rosario era)
- **Mario Bustos** — 12 appearances (1949–1950)
- **Enzo Valentino** — 7 appearances (1949–1951)
- **Omar Carranza** — 6 appearances (1961–1966)
- **Ignacio Díaz** — 4 appearances (debut session, April 10, 1944)
- **Rubén Maciel** — 4 solo + 4 duo appearances (1960–1966)
- **Antonio Rodríguez Lesende** — 4 appearances (1953, with Stampone)
- **Hugo Roca** — 3 (1949–1950); **Carlos Valdéz** — 3 (1951); **Dante Rossi** — 2 (1956); **Julio Martel** — 1; **Horacio Roca** — 1; **Jorge Casal** — 1 (1965); **Julia De Alba** — 2 duo appearances (1952)
- **Instrumental** — 121 appearances

**Reissue verification:** Year=2021 entries are digital remasters for streaming platforms (e.g., "Archivo RCA: La Década del '50" originally released as CD in 2004). Songs with no year data likely fall in the 1947–1953 period where Palazzo's blog volumes have gaps.

## 5. Alfredo De Angelis: all 486 Odeón recordings verified, 1969–1977 sessions are genuine

## 6. Miguel Caló's post-Golden-Age recordings are all authentic new sessions

**Total recordings: approximately 360**, spanning 1933–1972. The TangoSparks discography (the most meticulous scholarly source) lists ~260+ tango/vals/milonga studio recordings; adding the ~36 foreign tour recordings (1965 Colombia/Chile), ~20 radio versions, and non-tango genre recordings brings the total to the commonly cited ~360.

**Recording span:** 1933 (Splendid, debated — possibly June/July 1933, not 1932) through 1972 (Embassy label, final recordings before his death on May 24, 1972)

**"Pedacito De Cielo" verification:** Confirmed as recorded **September 2, 1942**, vals, singer Alberto Podestá, on Odeón. Composers: Enrique Francini & Héctor Stamponi. Year=1999 is definitively a reissue.

**Critical finding — ALL 1963, 1964, 1967, 1969 recordings are GENUINE:**
- **1963 (April–June):** The famous "Orquesta de las Estrellas" **reunion** — 12 new studio recordings on Odeón, reuniting Francini, Federico, Pontier, Baralis, and Trípodi with singers Berón and Podestá. Specific dates: April 16 ("En la calle," Berón), April 19 ("Qué falta que me hacés," Podestá; "Brazo de oro," instrumental), through June 7.
- **1963 (Nov–Dec):** 4 additional new recordings with Roberto Luque and Luis Correa
- **1964 (March):** 6 recordings with **Ranko Fujisawa** (Japanese singer — unmistakably new, not reissues). Plus 2 instrumental re-recordings (April 1964).
- **1966:** 14 recordings with Roberto Rufino and Miguel Montero
- **1967 (May):** 8 recordings with Alberto Marino; (September) 4 with Raúl Ledesma; plus instrumentals
- **1969 (March–May):** 8 instrumental recordings — last Odeón sessions
- **1972:** ~24 final recordings on **Embassy** label with Podestá, Iriarte, and Arrieta

## 7. Osvaldo Pugliese's ~383 studio recordings span five labels and 44 years

**Total studio recordings: approximately 383**, per TangoSparks's meticulous complete table. Including live recordings (Teatro Colón 1985, Amsterdam 1989), the total exceeds 400. Pugliese recorded for **Odeón (1943–1960), Stentor (1960, his own label, ~15 recordings), Philips (1961–1970, ~100+ recordings), and Odeón/EMI Odeón (1972–1987)**.

**Recording span:** July 15, 1943 ("El Rodeo," instrumental; "Farol," Roberto Chanel) through ~1987 ("Tokio Luminoso," "Compañera," "Igual Que Una Sombra"). Last live recording: June 29, 1989, Amsterdam Carré Theatre.

**"Sentencia" verification:** Confirmed as recorded **July 24, 1959**, tango, singer Ricardo Medina, on Odeón. Year=2011 is definitively a digital reissue date.

**"Year=1952-A" explanation:** The "-A" is a **matrix suffix designation** that was incorrectly parsed into the year field. Odeón used matrix suffixes like "/1" for re-recordings (e.g., "Barro" mx.18024 in May 1951 → mx.18024/1 in December 1952). The "-A" follows this convention, indicating an alternative take or pressing variant. The actual year is simply **1952**.

## Conclusion: what this research reveals

**Every suspicious date in the database is a confirmed reissue artifact.** Malerba's 2017 entries, Federico's 2021 entries, Donato's 2016 "La shunca," Lomuto's 2015 "Cicatrices," De Angelis's 2014 "Obsesión," Caló's 1999 "Pedacito De Cielo," and Pugliese's 2011 "Sentencia" all trace to digital streaming platform release dates contaminating recording-date metadata.

**The most significant factual correction concerns Ricardo Malerba.** His actual discography contains only **43 recordings on Odeón and Music Hall** — not 150 on RCA Victor. This three-fold overestimate and wrong label likely originate from confusion with "Reliquias" reissue CDs on BMG/RCA.

**Post-Golden-Age recordings are genuine for all three questioned directors.** De Angelis recorded continuously for Odeón through January 1977 (486 total), then Microfón/Columbia through 1985. Caló's 1963 "Orquesta de las Estrellas" reunion produced 12 real new recordings, and he continued through 1972 on Embassy. Pugliese never stopped — his five-label career extended through 1987 studio sessions and 1989 live performances.

**For complete track-by-track listings beyond what's presented here**, the three most reliable public sources are: el-recodo.com (requires contributor access for full data), Héctor Palazzo's blog at tangoradioymashistorias.blogspot.com (free, covers Malerba, Federico, De Angelis in extraordinary detail), and TangoSparks at tangosparks.com (free, the definitive source for Caló and Pugliese with pitch-calibrated data and era classifications).
